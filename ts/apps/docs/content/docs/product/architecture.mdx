---
title: How Sunred works
description: Feed formats, background refresh, search, and content safety — what affects your reading.
---

Sunred fetches feeds, parses them, and stores entries in the background. You don't need to manage any of it, but a few details are worth knowing because they shape what you see.

## Supported feed formats

Sunred reads the three formats sites actually publish:

| Format    | What it is                                  |
| --------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| RSS 2.0   | The most common format for blogs and news.  |
| Atom 1.0  | An older, XML-based standard.               |
| JSON Feed | A modern, JSON-based format.                |

Detection is automatic. Whichever format a site publishes, Sunred normalizes it into the same shape — title, link, content, author, date, tags, and any media attachments.

## Background refresh

Instead of checking every feed on a fixed clock, Sunred adapts the refresh interval to each feed. It looks at how often the feed publishes and checks it on a matching schedule — a daily blog is checked roughly daily, an hourly news feed more often. This keeps frequent publishers fresh without wasting requests on quiet ones.

If a feed fails to fetch or parse, Sunred retries on a shorter interval. The feed page shows the last error until the next refresh succeeds; otherwise you don't have to think about it. You can always fetch new entries on demand with the **Refresh** button on a feed's page.

## Content safety

Entry content comes from external sites, so Sunred sanitizes the HTML before storing it. Scripts and unsafe markup are stripped; links are forced to open safely and `javascript:` or `data:` URLs are removed. What you read is the article's text and images, not anything the feed's author could sneak in.

## Full-text search

Entry titles and content are indexed for search, so the search box in the sidebar can find articles by any word in the title or body. Search uses English-language matching.
